# Contributing

Thank you for your interest in contributing to the SD Standard.

The SD Standard is a living and evolving framework built on collaboration. It is designed to grow through contributions from designers, researchers, developers, educators, sustainability practitioners, suppliers, accessibility experts, students, and organizations working toward more responsible design practices.

This project includes more than code. Contributions may include improvements to the criteria, Knowledge Base, methodology, scoring models, tools, data structures, case studies, translations, project documentation, design assets, and technical infrastructure.

## About the SD Standard

The SD Standard helps designers reduce environmental harm, improve social outcomes, support cultural sustainability, and strengthen financial resilience through better design decisions.

The framework is organized around four pillars:

* **Environment:** natural resources, emissions, waste, energy, water, materials, and ecological impact
* **Society:** accessibility, labor, health, safety, equity, human rights, education, and public benefit
* **Culture:** local relevance, Indigenous knowledge, endangered languages, cultural diversity, and community participation
* **Finance:** fair compensation, economic benefit, accountability, transparency, and long-term value

The SD Standard is intended to support both practical design decisions and more transparent ways to evaluate, document, and improve the impact of communication design projects.

## Who can contribute

Contributions are welcome from people with different types of knowledge and experience, including:

* designers, UX designers, developers, writers, editors, and translators
* researchers, academics, sustainability specialists, and lifecycle assessment professionals
* accessibility experts, social health advocates, policy advisors, lawyers, and attorneys
* Indigenous knowledge holders and cultural practitioners
* schools, nonprofits, design associations, government agencies, and incubators
* printers, paper suppliers, digital hosting providers, accessibility vendors, packaging suppliers, product vendors, and certification bodies
* students, educators, and practitioners testing the SD Standard in real projects

You do not need to be a developer to contribute.

## Ways to contribute

You can contribute to the SD Standard in many ways, including:

* improving the clarity and usefulness of the criteria
* expanding the Knowledge Base
* contributing research, sources, examples, and case studies
* identifying gaps, risks, or missing perspectives
* testing the criteria on real or hypothetical projects
* improving scoring models and evidence requirements
* documenting baselines, project examples, and improved scenarios
* translating or localizing content
* improving accessibility, usability, and readability
* improving the website, tools, calculators, forms, or data structures
* reporting bugs or suggesting improvements
* helping create pathways for recognition, verification, or certification

## Content and Knowledge Base contributions

Content contributions help make the SD Standard more useful, understandable, and applicable to real design work.

Examples of content contributions include:

* Knowledge Base entries
* definitions and terminology
* references and research summaries
* examples of how a criterion can be applied
* case studies
* project documentation templates
* baseline examples
* scoring guidance
* supplier documentation guidance
* diagrams, worksheets, and teaching materials

Content contributions should be clear, practical, and evidence-informed. Where possible, please include sources, examples, or documentation that support the proposed contribution.

When contributing content, consider whether the material helps designers answer questions such as:

* What does this criterion mean in practice?
* Why does it matter?
* How can it be applied to a design project?
* What evidence or documentation would support a claim?
* What tradeoffs, limitations, or risks should be considered?
* How does it connect to the four pillars of the SD Standard?

## Research and methodology contributions

Research and methodology contributions help strengthen the credibility and usefulness of the SD Standard.

Examples include:

* reviewing criteria for accuracy, clarity, and relevance
* advising on scoring methods
* improving evidence requirements
* contributing environmental, social, cultural, or financial benchmarks
* reviewing alignment with existing standards or frameworks
* identifying limitations or unintended consequences
* helping validate assessment methods
* suggesting ways to distinguish achieved, estimated, modelled, and verified impact
* advising on recognition, verification, or certification pathways

Research contributions should be transparent about assumptions, limitations, and sources. If a contribution is based on a specific discipline, geography, sector, or methodology, please make that context clear.

## Design contributions

Design contributions help make the SD Standard easier to understand, use, and share.

Examples include:

* improving page layouts, navigation, and visual hierarchy
* creating diagrams, icons, templates, or explanatory graphics
* improving accessibility and readability
* designing worksheets, forms, reports, or project documentation templates
* improving the user experience of assessment tools
* creating visual systems for criteria, pillars, scoring, or project records
* testing how designers interact with the framework

Design contributions should support accessibility, clarity, and practical use. Where possible, design changes should be tested with real users or reviewed in relation to accessibility and usability principles.

## Code contributions

Code contributions are welcome, especially improvements that make the SD Standard website, tools, documentation, and data easier to use, maintain, or extend.

Examples of code contributions include:

* fixing bugs
* improving website performance
* improving mobile responsiveness
* improving accessibility
* improving navigation or search
* developing or refining assessment tools
* improving calculators, forms, or data visualizations
* updating scripts that generate documentation
* improving data structures for criteria, terms, scoring, or project records
* adding tests or improving build reliability
* improving developer documentation

Before making a substantial code change, please open an issue or discussion describing the proposed change and why it is needed.

## Editing generated documentation

Some documentation pages may be generated from source files. Please do not edit generated files directly unless the repository documentation specifically says to do so.

Before editing a file, check whether the content is managed through:

* Markdown source files
* criteria or terms JSON files
* documentation generation scripts
* generated output files

If a file includes a warning such as:

```txt
AUTO-GENERATED FILE. DO NOT EDIT DIRECTLY.
```

update the source file instead of editing the generated output.

This helps prevent contributions from being overwritten when the documentation is regenerated.

## GitHub workflow

For most contributions, please use the following workflow:

1. Check existing issues and pull requests to see whether the change has already been suggested.
2. Open an issue if you want to propose a new feature, major content change, methodology change, or technical improvement.
3. Fork the repository.
4. Create a new branch for your contribution.
5. Make your changes.
6. Test the project locally, if applicable.
7. Submit a pull request with a clear description of what changed and why.

Pull requests should be focused and limited in scope whenever possible. A smaller pull request is usually easier to review than one that changes many unrelated parts of the project.

## Pull request guidelines

When submitting a pull request, please include:

* a short summary of the change
* the reason for the change
* any related issue numbers
* screenshots or screen recordings for visual changes, when helpful
* notes about testing, if applicable
* sources or references for content or methodology changes
* any known limitations or follow-up work

For content, research, or methodology changes, please explain the reasoning behind the proposed update. For technical changes, please describe how the change was tested.

## Review process

Contributions may be reviewed for:

* alignment with the purpose of the SD Standard
* clarity and usefulness for designers
* accessibility and readability
* evidence quality
* technical reliability
* consistency with the four pillars
* transparency about assumptions and limitations
* cultural relevance and respect for lived experience
* compatibility with existing criteria, tools, and documentation

Some contributions may require discussion or revision before they can be accepted. This is especially true for changes to scoring methodology, criteria language, verification logic, or public claims about impact.

## Contribution principles

All contributions should support the SD Standard’s purpose: helping designers reduce environmental harm, improve social outcomes, support cultural sustainability, and strengthen financial resilience.

Contributions should aim to be:

* clear and practical
* evidence-informed
* accessible to non-specialists
* transparent about assumptions and limitations
* respectful of cultural context and lived experience
* compatible with the four pillars of the SD Standard
* useful for designers working on real projects

The SD Standard is not intended to be static. Criteria, scoring methods, guidance, tools, and examples may evolve as new research, technologies, regulations, and design practices emerge.

## Getting involved

If you are interested in contributing, collaborating, advising, testing, or partnering with the SD Standard, please open an issue, start a discussion, or get in touch through the project website.

You can contribute as an individual, organization, educator, researcher, supplier, student, or partner. Contributions can be small and specific, or part of a larger collaboration.

The SD Standard grows through shared knowledge, practical testing, and the collective effort of people working to make design more sustainable, equitable, culturally aware, and financially responsible.
