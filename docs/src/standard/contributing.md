# Contributing

The SD Standard is a living and evolving framework built on collaboration.

It is designed to grow through the contributions of designers, researchers, educators, suppliers, sustainability practitioners, accessibility experts, students, and organizations working toward more responsible design practices.

Contributions may include improving the criteria, expanding the Knowledge Base, testing tools, documenting projects, developing datasets, reviewing methodology, translating content, or helping create pathways for education, recognition, verification, and certification.

## Why contribute?

Sustainable design cannot be defined by one organization, region, discipline, or point of view. It requires shared knowledge, practical testing, and ongoing revision.

Contributing to the SD Standard helps:

* improve the clarity and usefulness of the criteria
* expand examples and case studies
* strengthen links between design practice and measurable impact
* support education and curriculum development
* improve accessibility and cultural relevance
* create more transparent methods for project evaluation
* develop stronger pathways for suppliers, partners, and verified projects

The goal is to make the SD Standard more useful, inclusive, evidence-based, and adaptable across different contexts.

## Contributors and collaborators

### For

Designers, developers, writers, translators, UX designers, researchers, students, and practitioners interested in sustainable communication design.

### Ways to contribute

Contributors and collaborators can help improve the SD Standard by:

* improving the user experience of the website and tools
* developing calculators, assessment tools, and project templates
* writing or editing Knowledge Base entries
* translating content
* creating case studies
* documenting sustainable design projects
* testing workflows and reporting methods
* creating datasets, examples, or visual explanations
* identifying gaps in the criteria or guidance

## Advisors and researchers

### For

Academics, sustainability specialists, accessibility experts, social health advocates, policy advisors, lawyers, attorneys, Indigenous knowledge holders, lifecycle assessment professionals, and subject-matter experts.

### Ways to contribute

Advisors and researchers can support the SD Standard by:

* reviewing criteria and methodology
* contributing research and references
* advising on scoring, verification, and evidence requirements
* helping validate frameworks and assessment approaches
* improving the Knowledge Base
* identifying risks, limitations, or missing perspectives
* advising on environmental, social, cultural, legal, or financial dimensions of design

## Educational and institutional partners

### For

Schools, universities, nonprofits, design associations, government agencies, incubators, and learning organizations.

### Collaboration opportunities

Educational and institutional partners can help bring the SD Standard into teaching, training, and public engagement through:

* sustainable design curriculum
* workshops and training sessions
* student challenges
* research partnerships
* certification pilots
* project documentation exercises
* regional adaptations
* public programs and exhibitions

These collaborations can help students and practitioners apply the SD Standard to real design decisions, while also contributing new examples, questions, and insights back into the framework.

## Industry and supply chain partners

### For

Printers, paper suppliers, digital hosting companies, accessibility vendors, packaging suppliers, lifecycle assessment providers, product vendors, certification bodies, and other suppliers connected to the design process.

### Ways to collaborate

Industry and supply chain partners can help make sustainable design decisions easier to understand, compare, and document by contributing:

* materials datasets
* environmental benchmarks
* verified supplier information
* low-impact production templates
* accessibility documentation
* product declarations
* lifecycle assessment guidance
* chain-of-custody systems
* evidence pathways for sustainable production

Supplier participation is especially important because many design decisions depend on the availability, transparency, and verification of materials, production methods, labor practices, and environmental data.

## Types of contributions

Contributions may include:

* **Content contributions:** Knowledge Base entries, definitions, examples, research summaries, case studies, and references.
* **Design contributions:** diagrams, templates, interface improvements, accessibility improvements, and communication materials.
* **Technical contributions:** website improvements, data structures, calculators, forms, APIs, and documentation tools.
* **Research contributions:** methodology review, scoring models, evidence standards, benchmarks, and impact measurement.
* **Educational contributions:** curriculum materials, workshop formats, teaching guides, and classroom pilots.
* **Translation and localization:** translated content, region-specific guidance, and culturally relevant examples.
* **Project documentation:** submitted projects, baselines, improved scenarios, lessons learned, and implementation notes.

## Contribution principles

All contributions should support the purpose of the SD Standard: helping designers reduce environmental harm, improve social outcomes, support cultural sustainability, and strengthen financial resilience.

Contributions should aim to be:

* clear and practical
* evidence-informed
* accessible to non-specialists
* transparent about assumptions and limitations
* respectful of cultural context and lived experience
* compatible with the four pillars of the SD Standard
* useful for designers working on real projects

When possible, contributors should include sources, examples, or documentation that help others understand and apply the contribution.

## Living framework

The SD Standard is not intended to be static. Criteria, scoring methods, guidance, tools, and examples may evolve as new research, technologies, regulations, and design practices emerge.

This means contributions may be used to:

* refine existing criteria
* add missing guidance
* improve scoring logic
* clarify evidence requirements
* update terminology
* improve accessibility
* identify new project types
* strengthen verification pathways
* support future certification systems

## Getting involved

If you are interested in contributing, collaborating, advising, testing, or partnering with the SD Standard, please get in touch.

You can contribute as an individual, organization, educator, researcher, supplier, student, or partner. Contributions can be small and specific, or part of a larger collaboration.

The SD Standard grows through shared knowledge, practical testing, and the collective effort of people working to make design more sustainable, equitable, culturally aware, and financially responsible.
