# F6: STANDARDS IMPROVEMENT — Extended Guidance

Related criterion: [F6: STANDARDS IMPROVEMENT](../../generated/criteria/FFP3.md)

Standards Improvement recognizes that sustainable design is not only about the outcome of a single project, but also about what the designing entity learns and improves because of the project. In the SD Standard, this criterion asks whether the project resulted in improvements to the designing entity’s documented standard procedures.

This criterion is important because many sustainability gains become more meaningful when they are repeated. A project may reveal a better supplier, a more efficient workflow, a lower-impact production method, a clearer accessibility checklist, a stronger contract clause, or a more inclusive research process. When those lessons are documented and added to the organization’s regular way of working, the impact can extend beyond one project and improve future work.

A project may meet this criterion when it leads to a specific update in the designing entity’s internal standards, templates, checklists, policies, onboarding materials, project management processes, quality-control steps, or evaluation methods. The improvement should be documented enough that it can be reused by the same team or shared with future collaborators.

## How to measure this criterion

1. A documented procedure was updated
The project resulted in a change to an internal process, checklist, template, brief, contract, production workflow, accessibility review, supplier selection process, or sustainability evaluation method.

2. The improvement is reusable
The change is not only a one-time project decision. It becomes part of the designing entity’s standard way of working and can be applied to future projects.

3. The reason for the change is clear
The team documented why the procedure was improved, such as reducing waste, improving accessibility, protecting workers, increasing transparency, reducing project risk, or improving financial sustainability.

4. The change is connected to evidence or project learning
The improvement is based on something learned during the project: a measured result, client feedback, supplier information, production issue, accessibility audit, post-project review, or team reflection.

5. The procedure is accessible to the team
The updated process is stored somewhere the team can realistically use it, such as a shared folder, project management system, internal wiki, onboarding guide, design system, or operations manual.
